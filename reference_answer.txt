GOLDEN ANSWER: Safety Requirements for Class II Medical Device Battery Housing
================================================================================
Reference Standards: IEC 60601-1:2012, FDA 21 CFR Part 820, ISO 14971:2019, ISO 13485:2016

1. ELECTRICAL SAFETY
   - The battery housing shall provide double insulation or reinforced insulation (Class II construction).
   - Patient leakage current shall not exceed 100 µA under normal conditions per IEC 60601-1.
   - Creepage and clearance distances shall be maintained for the rated working voltage.
   - The housing shall prevent access to live parts during normal use and single-fault conditions.

2. THERMAL SAFETY
   - The battery housing shall limit surface temperatures to 43°C for parts intended to contact the patient.
   - Thermal runaway protection shall be provided via battery management system (BMS).
   - Ventilation or thermal interface materials shall be included if battery heat dissipation exceeds 5W.

3. MECHANICAL SAFETY
   - The housing shall withstand a 1-meter drop test per IEC 60601-1 without compromising battery integrity.
   - Battery retention features shall prevent displacement during vibration and shock.
   - Enclosure integrity shall meet the IP rating required for the intended use environment.

4. BATTERY MANAGEMENT
   - Overcharge protection shall terminate charging at 100% ± 2% state of charge.
   - Short-circuit protection shall limit fault current within 1 second.
   - Over-discharge protection shall disconnect the load at the manufacturer-specified minimum voltage.

5. ELECTROMAGNETIC COMPATIBILITY (EMC)
   - The battery housing and associated circuitry shall comply with IEC 60601-1-2 for emissions and immunity.
   - Shielding or filtering shall be provided to prevent EMI from affecting device performance.

6. MATERIAL AND CHEMICAL SAFETY
   - Housing materials shall be non-toxic and chemically resistant to battery electrolytes.
   - Patient-contacting surfaces shall meet ISO 10993 biocompatibility requirements where applicable.

7. LABELING AND DOCUMENTATION
   - The housing shall bear legible warnings, battery type, polarity, and disposal instructions per FDA 21 CFR 801.
   - Instructions for use shall include safe handling, replacement, and disposal procedures.

8. RISK MANAGEMENT
   - Battery hazards shall be analyzed per ISO 14971:2019 and mitigated to acceptable risk levels.
   - A fault tree analysis (FTA) or failure modes and effects analysis (FMEA) shall document battery-related failure modes.

9. REGULATORY COMPLIANCE
   - The device shall comply with FDA 21 CFR Part 820 Quality System Regulation.
   - Lithium battery transportation shall meet UN 38.3 testing requirements.
   - Third-party safety certifications (UL, ETL, CE) shall be obtained as required by target markets.
